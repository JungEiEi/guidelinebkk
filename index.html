Historical & Cultural Heritage

Rattanakosin : The Soul of Thailand

Grand Palace Bangkok / Wat Phra Kaew

The Grand Palace consists of a complex of royal halls, buildings, and palaces constructed in exquisite traditional Thai architecture, showcasing remarkable craftsmanship and artistic detail. Within its grounds is Wat Phra Si Rattana Satsadaram (the Temple of the Emerald Buddha), the royal temple of the Grand Palace, which enshrines the Phra Phuttha Maha Mani Rattana Patimakorn (the Emerald Buddha), the most revered Buddha image and a cherished national symbol of Thailand. The buildings within the Grand Palace are arranged in an orderly manner, with the Inner Court clearly designated as the private residential area for members of the royal family.

The Golden Mount (Wat Saket)

It is one of the city's best-known viewpoints. The artificial hill was created during the reign of King Rama III. A large chedi was originally planned but collapsed due to the soft ground. The hill was later stabilized, and the current golden chedi was completed during the reign of King Rama V. From the top, you'll get panoramic views of Bangkok, especially impressive around sunrise, sunset, or after rain when the air is clearer.

Wat Arun Ratchawararam

Wat Arun Ratchawararam is regarded as a center of faith and an important symbol of both the Thonburi and Rattanakosin eras. The temple houses an ordination hall (Ubosot) enshrining a Buddha image in the Mara-Vijaya posture, along with various viharns and pagodas. Its entrance gates are adorned with the famous white and green guardian giants of Wat Arun, which serve as protective deities and reflect the temple’s rich beliefs and artistic beauty. In addition, Wat Arun is a popular destination for tourists who come to admire its architectural splendor and learn about its historical significance.
